## What

menuz asks macOS for notification authorisation at its first launch (`native/macos/Menuz/Menuz/Services/Notifier.swift:18`), and on macOS 27 that first request is an alert only the user can answer. While the alert is up, a notice the app posts from its own process, through `Notifier.post` (`Notifier.swift:135`), has never been measured. So it is not known whether the centre holds it until the alert is answered, refuses it as not yet allowed, or drops it, nor whether menuz's log says which.

The app's own posts are a run that could not start (`Runner.swift:66`), a run refused because the run id store could not be read (`MenuzApp.swift:305`), and a log write failure (`AppLog.swift:31`). The placement notice waits for the grant (`MenuzApp.swift:76`). A run's notice is posted by a separate process, the wrapper's `"$MENUZ_EXEC" --notify` (`native/macos/Menuz/MenuzKit/Sources/MenuzKit/Run.swift:130`), and its not-yet-allowed and off lines under the alert were seen in ST0027's hand runs.

What the ruling rests on, read at `1d0bb15`: `Notifier.post` (`native/macos/Menuz/Menuz/Services/Notifier.swift:135-146`) writes every outcome other than posted to menuz.log, with the notice's text: a refusal as not yet allowed or denied, and any other error as failed. So a notice the centre refuses, or fails to take, under the first launch's alert is logged rather than lost in silence. A notice the centre accepts and then never shows is the one case the app cannot see, and no change to menuz could log it. The case arises once per bundle id, at its first launch.

## Summary

`utilz doctor` splits its report across two streams. The banner, the step titles and the summary go to stdout. The verdict of every check (`✓ UTILZ_HOME=...`, and every warning, error and note) goes to stderr, because `run_doctor` prints verdicts through the shared message helpers (`opt/utilz/lib/common.sh:35-49`), which write to stderr. So `utilz doctor > report.txt` saves every step title and none of the answers, and `utilz doctor | less` pages the titles while the verdicts go straight to the terminal. hv ruled on 2026-09-14 (hv's board, decision 1) that doctor's report goes to stdout, by one redirect where the doctor verb runs, and that the shared helpers stay on stderr.

`utilz emacs doctor` has the same split. The ruling does not name it. It is included here because it is the same defect in the other doctor verb and takes the same one-redirect fix.

## Measured 2026-09-14

On this checkout, with every check passing:

- `utilz doctor`: 19 lines on stdout (the banner, the seven step titles, blank lines and the summary) and 7 on stderr (one verdict per check).
- `utilz emacs doctor`: 20 lines on stdout and 4 on stderr (its verdicts).

No consumer reads either report by stream: CI, the scripts and the docs only run it.

## Fix

- `bin/utilz` runs each verb with its stderr sent to stdout: `run_doctor 2>&1` where `doctor` dispatches, and `emacs_doctor 2>&1` where `emacs doctor` does. The report comes out whole, in order, on one stream, and the exit code is unchanged. The shared helpers keep writing to stderr for every other caller.
- Red-first tests at the dispatcher, where the redirect lives: `opt/utilz/test/dispatcher.bats` for `utilz doctor` and `opt/utilz/test/bridge.bats` for `utilz emacs doctor`. Each runs the verb twice. With stdout discarded, stderr must be empty. With stderr discarded, a verdict line must be on stdout, so an empty report cannot pass. The existing tests cannot tell the streams apart, because BATS `run` merges them.

## Resolution

Fixed in two commits under this issue.

- 363d9a7 adds the two tests, red first. Against 611314a under bash 3.2.57, both failed on their verdict: "UTILZ_HOME=" for `utilz doctor`, and "Canonical elisp present" for `utilz emacs doctor`. Through the dispatcher, the two verbs put 7 and 4 verdict lines on stderr.
- The commit that closes this issue adds `2>&1` where each verb runs in `bin/utilz`. Measured under bash 3.2.57, 18:49-18:52Z: both tests pass. Through the dispatcher, `utilz doctor` now puts 26 lines on stdout and none on stderr, and `utilz emacs doctor` puts 24 and none. The exit code is unchanged: 0 with every check passing, and 1 with yq off `PATH`, where the error verdicts now reach stdout. `utilz test utilz` passes, and CI's shellcheck step, run as written, is clean over its 18 files.

`utilz emacs doctor` was fixed on the ruling's principle rather than its text: the ruling names `utilz doctor`, and the emacs verb is the same defect in the other doctor verb.
